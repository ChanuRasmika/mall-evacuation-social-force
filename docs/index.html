Shopping-mall evacuation: Social Force Model demo

A 40 m × 30 m mall floor with six shop blocks and two exits. Each person is pushed towards the nearest exit and pushed away from other people and walls. Change the crowd size, the exit width and the walking speed, and see whether the floor empties within the 3-minute target.

Scenario

The same seed gives the same crowd and the same run.

Playback

Runs seeds 1000–1009 with the current settings, without drawing, and reports the mean evacuation time with a 95 % confidence interval.

About / How to use

Model. Each person is a disc of radius 0.25–0.35 m with their own desired walking speed (normal distribution, s.d. 0.26 m/s). A driving force pulls them along the shortest walking route to the nearest open exit, computed once per layout with the fast marching method. Repulsion, body compression and sliding friction act between people and between people and walls (Helbing, Farkas and Vicsek, 2000). The equations of motion are integrated with Velocity-Verlet with a velocity predictor, at a step of 0.005 s (0.0025 s above 400 people). Cell lists find the neighbours of each person. Someone counts as evacuated 0.5 m past an exit.

  • Set the scenario and press Start. Pause stops the clock, and Reset starts again with the current settings. Changing a setting resets the run.
  • People are coloured by their current speed: red is stopped or squeezed, blue is walking freely. The dashed half-circles mark where the density at each exit is measured.
  • The chart shows the number of people still inside against time, with the 180 s target as a dashed line.
  • Flow per exit is the number of people per second that left through each exit over the last 5 s. Specific flow, shown at the end, is people per metre of exit width per second while 20–80 % of the crowd leaves.
  • Run 10 seeds gives the mean evacuation time with a 95 % confidence interval. If the settings match a case simulated in the notebook, it is compared with the notebook's result (20 seeds). The two use different random number generators, so they agree statistically, not run for run: the check passes if the means differ by less than the two confidence half-widths added together.
  • Reference case: 100 people, 1.5 m exits, 1.34 m/s, both exits open. The notebook gives 38.8 ± 0.9 s.

Limitations. Exits narrower than about 1.0 m may never clear: in this model the slowest people stop at the door edges once nobody is pushing from behind. Everyone starts moving at once (no reaction time) and goes to the nearest exit, even if it is crowded. Large crowds are slow to simulate: a batch of 10 runs at 800 people takes several minutes.
